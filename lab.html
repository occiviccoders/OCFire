How scoring works, and what the terms mean

Alert levels

Each unconfirmed heat spot gets a points total from the signals below. 5 or more points is Elevated, 3–4 is Moderate, anything lower is Low. That's what "Elevated (5+)" and "Moderate (3+)" on the charts mean. It's a ranking, not a probability.

Terms

Track
One heat spot followed over time. Detections within about 1¼ miles of each other are grouped into the same track.
Pass
One satellite flying over (or, for GOES, one scan). Polar satellites pass a few times a day; GOES scans every 5–10 minutes.
Detection
One satellite pixel flagged as hot. A fire usually lights up several pixels per pass.
FRP
Fire radiative power: how much heat the fire gives off, in megawatts (MW). Under 5 MW is small (a burn pile or a fire's edge); 20+ MW is a strong, active fire.
Collector
Our background job. Every 15 minutes it pulls the latest satellite data from NASA and scores each track. "We logged it" is when it got the data, usually 1–3 hours after the satellite actually saw the heat, because NASA needs time to process each pass. The first pass is the real first sighting.
Outcome
Reported fire: matched to a CAL FIRE or NIFC incident within 3 miles. No report: 72 hours with no new heat and no incident. Recurring: a spot that's hot most days (farms, flares). On a known fire: started next to a fire that was already reported.
Lead time
How long before the agency's listed start time we had rated the track Moderate. Positive means we were earlier.
Hit rate
Of the tracks that are settled, the share that turned out to be a reported fire.

Scorecard
